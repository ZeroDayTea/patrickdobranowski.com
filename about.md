I am a student at Vanderbilt University as part of the [Ingram Scholars Program](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/scholarships/ingram.php). I've worked at [SpaceX](https://www.spacex.com/), [Trail of Bits](https://www.trailofbits.com/), the [ELI-ALPS Laser Research Institute](https://www.eli-alps.hu/), and [Vanderbilt University's Institute for Software Integrated Systems](https://www.isis.vanderbilt.edu/), and I currently help instruct and assist for CS4277: CyberSecurity at Vanderbilt.

My academic and professional interests span *systems, security, programming languages, and pure mathematics*. I especially enjoy low-level low-latency programming, systems and systems security, embedded systems, robotics, machine learning, simulation software, type theory, and algebra/group theory with an eye towards making computers *go really fast*.

I compete in CTF cybersecurity competitions with [FMC](https://ctf.mt/) and [.;,;.](https://ctf.gg/), and have been invited to events all around the US and as far as Switzerland and Saudi Arabia. I've also given guest lectures at conferences and meetups in New York and my family's home city, Kraków, Poland.

My current research focus is real-time systems, particularly real-time networking in trusted execution environments. Previously, I worked on quantum optics and quantum electrodynamics constructing quantum models of high harmonic generation and developing relevant simulation software at ELI-ALPS. Prior to that, I worked on privacy-preserving machine learning developing DNN techniques for detecting deepfake audio content.

Outside of academics and research, I'm passionate about linguistis and philosophy, and I enjoy climbing, mountaineering, wildlife conservation, and speedcubing.
